# Apex must pay to serve teens AI

No free AI provider both clearly allows a site used by 13-17 year olds and is smart enough for Apex Scholar's hardest tasks. The fix is to leave Gemini and pay Anthropic a small bill with a hard cap. Google's Gemini API terms ban any service "likely to be accessed by" under-18s, on paid and unpaid tiers alike, and Vertex AI repeats the ban. Apex's current setup therefore breaks its provider's terms today. The 11-key rotation is a second breach if the keys sit in separate projects. Three free routes get past the age clauses, and each has a catch. Cloudflare Workers AI gives about 400 calls a day on a model too weak for answer keys and photo grading. Hack Club AI's rules appear to require every end user to be 18 or under. Puter has not documented how it handles AI data. Claude Haiku 5.5 costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output. Anthropic allows teen end users without parental consent, which OpenAI requires, and does not train on API data. At Apex's real load of about 5 monthly active users, that comes to a few dollars a month. Even the full 12,000-call daily design capacity would cost roughly $130-400 a month. Haiku's weak spot is vision: nothing shows it reads photos well, so photo grading needs an Apex-run test first. Separately, Colorado's chatbot law takes effect on January 1, 2027, before the March-May peak. Its proposed rules ban badges and login streaks for minors, which hits Apex's achievements directly.

## Google's ban covers every Gemini tier, so Apex breaks its terms today

The Gemini API Additional Terms (effective March 23, 2026) say users must be 18 or older. They also bar using the APIs in a site directed at, or **likely to be accessed by, individuals under 18** ([Gemini API Additional Terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). That clause sits in the general terms, not the unpaid-tier section, so paying Google changes nothing. Vertex AI has its own version. Cloud Service Terms s.20(d) says the customer will not, and will not let end users, use any Generative AI Service in such a site, and s.20(f) allows **immediate suspension** on a suspected violation ([Google Cloud Service Terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms)). Google Cloud credits cannot fix this either, for the same reason. In October 2026 a tutoring business asked on Google's developer forum whether the API can serve under-18 students. The only reply, with no staff badge visible, declined to answer and pointed back to the terms ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/can-gemini-api-be-used-in-an-educational-platform-for-students-under-18/190152)).

The unpaid tier adds a data problem on top of the age ban. Google uses unpaid-tier content to improve its products, **human reviewers may read it**, and Google tells developers not to submit personal information ([Gemini API Additional Terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). A photo of a 15-year-old's handwritten chemistry work is exactly what that warning covers.

The 11 rotating keys carry their own risk. Gemini rate limits apply **per project, not per API key** ([Gemini rate limits](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/rate-limits)). So rotating keys within one project gains nothing, and spreading them across projects to multiply quota is the plain reading of the Google APIs Terms' ban on attempts to circumvent documented limits, s.2(d) ([Google APIs Terms](https://developers.google.com/terms)). No Google text names multi-project pooling outright. But forum reports describe automated abuse flags, project-wide quota set to zero, and a reply that Google "cannot lift the automated restrictions on free tier accounts" ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-api-project-wide-quota-set-to-0-rpm-after-policy-notice-appeal-still-pending/176359)). The realistic failure is every linked project going dark at once, perhaps in April. The research did not check whether Apex's keys share one project.

The ban also follows Gemini into indirect routes. OpenRouter's terms make developers ensure their users comply with each model's own terms (§5.1). Its free Gemma 4 variants, which are its only free vision options besides NVIDIA's, are served through Google AI Studio ([OpenRouter terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms); [OpenRouter endpoints API](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it:free/endpoints)). Puter's Gemini-family models, such as `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, pass Google's terms through to Puter as well ([Puter chat docs](https://docs.puter.com/AI/chat/)).

**Cloudflare Workers AI** comes closest to a clean yes. Its Self-Serve agreement and developer-platform terms set no age bar and make the customer solely responsible for its end users. Cloudflare's privacy policy does say its services are not intended for under-18s, but the same policy says it does not apply to customers' own sites ([Cloudflare service terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-developer-platform/); [Cloudflare privacy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/)). Its data commitment is the strongest in the field: standard inference keeps **no inputs or outputs** after the response is delivered, and nothing is used for training ([Workers AI data usage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/data-usage/)). It also runs inside the Worker through the AI binding, which avoids the IP blocking Groq has applied at its Cloudflare edge ([Groq community](https://community.groq.com/t/getting-access-denied/886/2)). The allowance is the problem. 10,000 neurons a day, resetting at midnight UTC, buys about **407 calls** on Gemma 4 26B at a 1,200-in, 500-out request. That is about 3% of design capacity, or roughly 31 practice tests. Qwen3.8 27B, the strongest model there, gets only about 51 calls a day ([Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). These figures assume that request size and ignore image tokens, so real capacity is lower.

**Hack Club AI** is the only service built for teens. It offers every OpenRouter model, with no credit card and **$3 a day** per account. Its rules say it is "for teens 18 and under only." They also bar building tools that let others reach the API "without them also abiding by these rules" ([Hack Club AI rules](https://docs.ai.hackclub.com/guide/rules.html)). Apex accepts users from 13 with no upper limit, including seniors who turn 18 and teachers. The developer is 18 and will age out. The upstream, OpenRouter, requires users to be 18 ([OpenRouter terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms)), and Hack Club's dashboard lets the account holder inspect what was sent. A short email asking whether a public, ad-free study site may proxy for its users would settle the question cheaply. Until Hack Club answers yes in writing, it cannot be the primary provider.

**Puter** moves the age question off Apex entirely, because each student signs in to their own Puter account, which pays for their own AI use. Puter's floor is 13 ([Puter terms](https://puter.com/terms)). But the free monthly allowance is not published, and Puter's terms and privacy policy predate its AI features. How it and its upstream vendors handle students' prompts is undocumented ([Puter user-pays](https://docs.puter.com/user-pays-model/)). Apex already has this as an opt-in path, which is the right role for it.

Free tiers are also shrinking. GitHub Models was **fully retired on July 30, 2026** ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-models)). Cerebras replaced its free tier with a $5 trial, according to a third-party tracker ([UsagePricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/cerebras)). Groq's published cap now reads 1K requests a day per model, against 14,400 quoted in guides from earlier in 2026 ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits); [eesel](https://eesel.ai/blog/groq-pricing)). A plan that depends on any free tier lasting through May is a gamble.

## Free models miss the bar exactly where answer keys and photos need it

Model quality knocks out most of the remaining free options. The useful independent signals are science reasoning (GPQA), hard multi-step physics (CritPt), printed-diagram vision (MMMU-Pro), and factual calibration (the Omniscience score, where negative means confidently wrong). Workers AI's cheapest vision model, **Gemma 4 26B, scores .792 on GPQA, .692 on MMMU-Pro and 0 on CritPt**. Gemma 4 31B, Apex's current overflow tier, has an Omniscience score of **-48.5 with an .855 hallucination rate**, the worst of any model in the comparison. Compare Gemini 3.8 Flash at .953 GPQA and .856 MMMU-Pro ([AA: Gemma 4 26B](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemma-4-26b-a4b); [AA: Gemma 4 31B](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemma-4-31b); [AA: Gemini 3.8 Flash](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-8-flash)). The best free-hostable model is **Qwen3.8 27B**: .905 GPQA, .763 MMMU-Pro and a low .286 hallucination rate ([AA: Qwen3.8 27B](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/qwen3-8-27b)). It is PRC-origin, though. Independent tests found DeepSeek models echoed four times as many misleading CCP narratives as US models, even when the weights ran outside China ([NIST CAISI](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2025/09/caisi-evaluation-deepseek-ai-models-finds-shortcomings-and-risks)). Qwen 3.5 gave poor answers on 86% of Taiwan-policy questions ([CEIAS](https://ceias.eu/chinese-llms-and-the-spillover-effects-of-political-alignment/)). That rules Qwen out for AP Comparative Government, World History and US Government. It remains usable for STEM and for transcription, where political distortion does not apply.

Claude Haiku 5.5, at its maximum effort setting, posts .444 on HLE (a hard reasoning benchmark), .189 on CritPt and an Omniscience score of 12.3 with a .342 hallucination rate ([AA: Claude Haiku 5.5](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-haiku-5-5)). That makes it the cheapest well-calibrated model in the comparison, which matters most for history and government tutoring. Two caveats are real. First, effort settings swing cheap models enormously: Haiku's index score runs from **18 at low effort to 36.5 at max** ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)), and reasoning tokens bill as output. Second, Haiku has **no MMMU-Pro score**, and the best Haiku entry on the human-vote Vision Arena ranks 128th, version unestablished ([Vision Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/chat/vision)). GPT-6 Luna, at the same price, has proven vision (.797 MMMU-Pro) but a .793 hallucination rate ([AA: GPT-6 Luna](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gpt-6-luna)). Luna is fit for reading a photo, not for stating history.

For Apex's highest-stakes tasks, the pipeline matters more than the model. In the only direct study of photographed STEM work, 600 university submissions, cheap vision models reached **89-99% rubric-item accuracy**. **87% of the best model's errors were transcription failures** from blur, rotation or missing work, and the pricier GPT-5.1 did worse than GPT-5-mini ([arXiv 2605.19043](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19043v1)). On typed short answers with a rubric, Claude Sonnet 4 reached a quadratic weighted kappa (QWK, an agreement score) of 0.88-0.90, against 0.90-0.92 between humans. GPT-5 scored only 0.77-0.79, so newer does not mean better ([JMIR Medical Education](https://mededu.jmir.org/2026/1/e89445)). For question generation, a meta-analysis put the pooled accuracy of LLM-written items at **0.73** ([PLOS One](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12758716/)). A 2026 preprint found models solve problems well yet struggle to produce valid hard ones ([arXiv 2605.06660](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06660v1)). That gap explains wrong answer keys better than model weakness does. It points to a fix that costs little: have a second model from a different vendor solve each generated question blind, keep only items where it lands on the key, and recompute numeric answers in code. For photos, transcribe first, show the student the transcription to confirm, then grade the confirmed text. No published study tests any November 2026 cheap model on AP free responses or teen handwriting, so every grading and photo choice here is provisional until Apex runs its own evaluation against AP Central's scored samples ([AP Central](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-biology/exam/past-exam-questions)).

## Anthropic's terms fit an open, guest-friendly teen site best

Among providers that expressly allow teen end users, the conditions differ in a way that matters for Apex's guest access. OpenAI's Services Agreement bars letting minors use its services **"without consent from their parent or guardian"** ([OpenAI Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/)). It also requires zero data retention, granted only with OpenAI's prior approval, before processing any under-13 personal data, and it recommends its "most current flagship models" for minor-facing products ([OpenAI under-18 guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/safety-checks/under-18-api-guidance)). Mistral and Kimi likewise require parental consent ([Mistral Commercial Terms](https://legal.mistral.ai/terms/commercial-terms-of-service); [Kimi terms](https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/agreement/modeluse)). A guest flow collects no consent, and none of these texts says how consent must be obtained. Anthropic sets **no parental-consent requirement and no age floor**. It requires safeguards suited to the use case, such as moderation, monitoring and age verification. It also requires COPPA compliance stated on the website and a disclosure that users are talking to an AI, and it audits periodically ([Anthropic: serving minors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9307344-responsible-use-of-anthropic-s-models-guidelines-for-organizations-serving-minors)). Its Commercial Terms bar training on customer content, and API data is deleted within 30 days by default ([Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms); [Claude privacy center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)). OpenAI's Usage Policies also list academic dishonesty as a prohibited use ([OpenAI Usage Policies](https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/)), which a photo solver that hands over finished homework sits close to. Anthropic's Usage Policy treats grading that decides certification as high-risk ([Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). Apex's practice scores are not that, but they should be labelled as practice estimates.

The money is small at real usage. Claude Haiku 5.5 costs **$0.10 input and $0.50 output per million tokens** for prompts under 100,000 tokens, with **50% off on the Batch API** ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). A typical call costs $0.00037 at 1,200 tokens in and 500 out, and $0.0011 at 3,000 in and 1,500 out.

| Daily calls | Haiku 5.5, small calls ($/month) | Haiku 5.5, large calls ($/month) | Workers AI Gemma 4 26B, paid overflow ($/month) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 (roughly today) | ~$1 | ~$3 | $0 (inside free allowance) |
| 1,000 | ~$11 | ~$33 | ~$4 |
| 12,000 (design capacity) | ~$133 | ~$396 | ~$94 plus Workers Paid fee |

Those figures are arithmetic from list prices ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). They exclude reasoning tokens, which can multiply output cost several times at high effort. The design capacity is the wrong anchor: 12,000 calls a day is about 2,400 per current active user. The budget should track measured use, with a hard daily spend cap. The developer has no company or revenue, so the grant routes are mostly closed. Cloudflare, AWS, NVIDIA and Microsoft startup credits all require an incorporated business ([Cloudflare for Startups](https://www.cloudflare.com/forstartups/); [NVIDIA Inception](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/startups/)). Anthropic gives new accounts only small testing credits. Hack Club's HCB fiscal sponsorship can accept tax-deductible donations for a 7% fee ([HCB](https://hackclub.com/fiscal-sponsorship/)), which is a realistic way for a few donors to cover a $10-30 monthly bill.

## Colorado's chatbot law lands in January, before the spring peak

Apex's legal exposure comes mainly from other states' chatbot laws, not Minnesota's. Minnesota's proposed chatbot ban for minors, SF 1857, had no education carve-out, but it never left the Senate ([Revisor status](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/bill.php?b=Senate&f=SF1857&ssn=0&y=2025)). Watch for a similar bill in January 2027. Minnesota's privacy law applies only from 100,000 Minnesota consumers ([325M.12](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/325M.12)). Colorado's HB26-1263, signed May 29, 2026, covers any public AI system that "primarily simulates human conversation". The research found no education or small-operator exemption, and duties start **January 1, 2027** ([Colorado General Assembly](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb26-1263)). Operators must estimate account holders' ages, disclose AI use, keep a self-harm protocol, file annual reports to the attorney general, and for known minors "avoid rewards that encourage engagement". The AG's proposed rules of August 11, 2026 go further. They say self-declaration alone is not enough for age estimation. They ban **"leaderboards, badges, login streaks, and features tied to session length"** for minors. And they default minors to no retention of prior sessions and no training on their data ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/08/colorado-proposes-rules-for-automated-decision-making-technology-and-chatbot-safety/)). Apex's `userAchievements` and streak features fall squarely within that ban. The rules are proposals, and whether a study site with a tutor tab "primarily" simulates conversation is arguable. But Apex cannot win that argument cheaply, and the features are easy to change.

The other laws are narrower. California's SB 243 and New York's companion law target bots built to meet social needs or ask emotion-based questions, which a study tutor probably is not. Oregon expressly excludes education ([SB 243](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB243/2025); [NY GBL 1700](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/oregon-and-washington-join-california-in-enacting-companion-chatbot-laws)). COPPA covers "commercial" operators, and whether a free, ad-free project run by an individual counts is unresolved. The FTC lets general-audience sites ask age neutrally and block under-13s ([FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)). These requirements overlap heavily with Anthropic's own: an AI disclosure, a crisis protocol, content filters, and a public COPPA statement satisfy both the laws and the provider. Apex has no parent or teacher account to alert, unlike Khanmigo, which emails a linked adult when it flags a conversation ([Khan Academy support](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/tr/articles/13871273505933)). The minimum credible substitute is a 988 referral plus a flag log the developer reviews.

### The recommended plan, in order

First, move the Worker's default provider from Gemini to **Claude Haiku 5.5** on Anthropic's API. Use high effort for tutoring and grading and low effort for flashcards. Set a hard daily spend cap in the Worker; $2 a day covers about 2,000-5,000 calls. When the cap is hit, fall back to Workers AI's free allowance for low-stakes, grounded work such as flashcards built from study-pack text. Show a clear "AI is busy" state rather than calling Gemini. The Netlify fallback cannot use the Workers AI binding and should call Anthropic directly. The repo's `CLAUDE.md` still describes the Netlify proxy and Gemini key manager as the main path, so it should be updated with the switch. Second, move question generation offline into batches, using Haiku at max effort on the half-price Batch API. Have Qwen3.8 27B on Workers AI solve each STEM question blind, and recompute numeric keys in code. A stored, verified question bank cuts live calls and fixes the key-error problem at its source. Third, before shipping photo grading on any model, test Haiku 5.5, Qwen3.8 27B (transcription only) and GPT-6 Luna on about 200 real photo cases, with a transcription-confirm step. Adopting Luna means accepting OpenAI's parental-consent requirement, so if only Luna passes, limit photo features to signed-in users with a consent flow. Fourth, keep Puter as an opt-in path, but hide its Gemini-family models. Email Hack Club asking whether Apex may proxy for its users. Fifth, before January 1, 2027, turn off streaks and badges for all users, since Apex cannot reliably tell who is a minor. Add a persistent AI disclosure, a 988 crisis protocol, a neutral 13+ age gate that deletes under-13 accounts on discovery, and a COPPA statement. Update the privacy policy to name Anthropic and Cloudflare as processors. Then retire the Gemini keys and their projects.

## Conclusion

The question assumed the constraint was money, but the real constraint is that "free" and "allowed for teens" barely overlap. Google charges nothing and bans the audience. OpenAI allows the audience but trades free tokens for training data. The providers with clean terms charge for every call. At Apex's actual traffic, the compliant bill is smaller than the cost of one suspended project in April. The expensive part of serving teens properly is the compliance work: age estimation under Colorado's rules, a crisis protocol, and redesigning gamification. Choosing Anthropic does not remove that work. It only keeps it from being blocked by the provider's terms.

The second shift is about where quality comes from. The evidence suggests Apex's answer-key and grading errors come more from pipeline design than from model choice. Most handwriting errors are transcription failures, and wrong keys come from generators that never solve their own questions. A blind cross-model solve and a student-confirmed transcription would likely improve accuracy more than an upgrade to a costlier model. Both can be measured on AP Central's scored samples before any money moves. Three open questions decide the edges of this plan: whether Hack Club says yes, whether Haiku 5.5 reads handwriting well enough, and whether Colorado's final rules keep the ban on streaks. None of them changes the first step, which is to stop sending teenagers' data to a provider that has banned them.
