# Film Apex Scholar on one moving stage

> Update (2026-10-22): the hero sim is now AP Psychology, "Let me condition Pavlov's dog" (chosen after testing five tutor sims), the graph is the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve, the counts are 37 AP courses and 28 interactive sims, and the end card says "Free and open source" with apex-scholar.com. The approved on-screen text lives in "Launch video script draft.md"; it replaces the text in the storyboard below.

Make the launch film a 56-second, music-led, text-only piece built in the Remotion project you already have. Render the app's **real React components** (SimWidget, GraphWidget, StepReveal, TestPanel, FrqAnswerBox, the results rubric, the score calculator, study-pack sims) on one dark stage, and let a moving camera, word-by-word headlines and live UI state changes carry the story instead of slide changes. The reference film, Sill's 83-second launch, drew **about 649K views on a channel with 3.37K subscribers**. It had no voiceover, no captions and one solo developer behind it, so this format does not depend on brand reach ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJnNkfHLdFw)). The whole pipeline can be **$0 with no API keys**. Remotion is free for an individual, including commercial use, and needs no paid renders when you render locally ([Remotion Terms v5.0](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/terms)). Mixkit music and SFX are cleared for commercial use with no attribution ([Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/llm-info/)). Captions come from local Whisper ([Remotion docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/install-whisper-cpp/)). The biggest danger is not cost. It is that viewers already call this exact style the "default AI trailer" look, and that AI grading gets overpromised. The plan below counters both by showing real outputs, including a rubric point the student *missed*. Effort is **about 2–3 weeks** of focused solo work for the hero film plus vertical cut-downs, or about 1 week on a reduced path.

## Sill feels like a film because the set never changes

The Sill film runs **about 30 beats in 83 seconds**, a new idea every 2–3 seconds, and the frame is never static even during a "hold" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJnNkfHLdFw)). Its main trick is a **single persistent set**. One iPad stays on stage from 0:10 to 0:34 while the camera pushes into the thumbnail bar, crops to the top edge, then pulls back to the full device. A slideshow changes pages; this film moves a camera. Text is a layer on that set, not a separate card: the UI dims and blurs under each headline ("Any Mac window.", "One tap to switch.") and then un-dims. Headlines build **word by word**, with pending words sitting dim before they brighten, and stacked lists grey out older lines as each new one lands. The film also syncs text and UI builds. For example, "Wi-Fi. Wired. Direct. Remote." appears one word at a time while matching rows appear in the real "Connect to a Mac" list beside it. Copy follows strict rules: **at most about six words per headline**, sentence case with a period, a small grey subline for the technical caveat, and one accent colour used once ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJnNkfHLdFw)).

Three moments did the persuading. First, features are shown working rather than described: the text-size slider moves and the text grows, and the window dims on the Mac while staying live on the iPad. Second, an **extreme close-up on one specific, true readout**, "Connected through Tailscale · 48 ms", is the frame commenters quoted back ("Alright lemme install this right now") ([YouTube comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJnNkfHLdFw)). Third, a surprising values close, "No subscription. No servers. Free and open source", drew the most-liked comments ([YouTube comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJnNkfHLdFw)). The structure goes feature → proof → value → brand → tease, with no problem-agitation act. The music was "written for this film", and the top comment praised the sound design ([YouTube description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJnNkfHLdFw)).

The warning sign is in the same comment section. Viewers speculated the film was AI-generated in a web app, called the style "uncanny", and one called it a default style they are tired of ([YouTube comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJnNkfHLdFw)). Copy Sill 1:1 and Apex Scholar inherits that reaction. The wider launch-film record points to the same fix. Vercel's v0 film makes the **prompt box the hero and shows the UI assembling live** ([Content Cabinet](https://www.contentcabinet.in/insights/product-launch-video-examples)). Devin's film earned credibility by **showing the product recovering from an error** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fjHtjT7GO1c)). AutoAE warns that faking the product with seeded data turns a launch video into a liability ([AutoAE](https://autoae.online/blog/best-product-launch-videos)). So take Sill's camera grammar, but take your content from real tutor generations and real grading output, including the imperfect parts.

## The concept: "Watch it build"

**Creative concept.** A student types one sentence to the Apex tutor, and the film never leaves the stage that sentence creates. The camera follows the answer as it assembles itself: a playable simulation forms inside the chat bubble, a graph draws its own curve, and a worked solution reveals one step per beat. Then the same stage slides sideways into a practice test laid out like the digital exam, where a photo of handwritten FRQ work drops in and comes back marked against the rubric. The camera stops on the one point the student *didn't* earn, and why. That close-up is the film's "Tailscale · 48 ms": proof that the grading is real, not flattery. A predicted score rolls on an odometer, study-pack sims flash past in a beat-synced montage, and the film closes on its own "wait, really?" line. Everything is **free**, it covers **37 AP courses**, and it was **built by a student**. Everything is dark, quiet and precise, and the product supplies all the colour. One accent colour appears exactly once, on the word "Free."

The structure follows the data. Wistia's 13M-video dataset puts sub-one-minute videos at a **52% average engagement rate** ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length)). Notion 3.0 had its pitch on screen by second 3 and the product by second 4 ([Content Cabinet](https://www.contentcabinet.in/insights/product-launch-video-examples)). Product Hunt guides converge on **30–60 seconds**, and Product Hunt requires the video as a YouTube link ([Screenhance](https://screenhance.com/blog/product-hunt-launch-video-guide)). The film below is 56 seconds, shows real UI by second 2, and makes one ask at the end. The "37 AP courses" figure comes from counting the `AP_SUBJECTS` entries in `ap-prep-hub/src/constants/subjects.js`. Before it goes on screen, confirm that every one of those courses has practice tests.

Two craft rules hold throughout. Every beat has continuous small motion (slow push, dim, cursor drift), and transitions are camera moves or `pushCut` / `zoomBlur` / `crossZoom` / `linearBlur` rather than fades. Remotion v4 ships all of those, and the HTML-in-canvas ones render headlessly with no setup ([Remotion html-in-canvas guide](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/html-in-canvas-guide.mdx); [TransitionSeries](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitions/transitionseries)). Add `<CameraMotionBlur>` only on fast moves and keep `samples` low, because the docs warn it is "destructive to colors" ([Remotion motion blur](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/motion-blur/camera-motion-blur)).

## Vertical cuts do the distribution; the hero does credibility

Polished hero films and lo-fi creator clips do different jobs. Edtech growth on TikTok has come from creator-led, native-feeling content about real student pain. Knowt reported **519% TikTok growth** after a single organic post took off and it went creator-first ([TikTok Academy](https://omg.tiktokacademy.com/student/page/3649444-how-knowt-used-tiktok-to-grow-519-in-a-single-year)). Gauth built on student creators making homework and exam-prep videos ([Home From College](https://homefromcollege.com/blog/case-studies/gauth)). Duolingo's less-polished, unhinged account is the canonical Gen Z example ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/duolingo-unhinged-content)). TikTok guidance stresses a hook in the first 3 seconds, 9:16, and captions with sound on ([Stackmatix](https://stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-creative-best-practices-2026)). A TikTok-commissioned study found **88% of users call sound vital** ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/evolution-of-sound-volume-1)). The hero film serves the landing page, Product Hunt, teachers and parents. The vertical clips are what spread.

Build vertical as a **second Remotion composition at 1080×1920** that reuses the same scene components with re-keyed camera moves. Do not centre-crop, because most of the UI is horizontal. Add word-by-word captions with `createTikTokStyleCaptions()` from `@remotion/captions` ([Remotion captions](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/captions/create-tiktok-style-captions)). YouTube Shorts allow up to 3 minutes since October 2024, so length is not the constraint ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/youtube-shorts-can-now-run-up-to-three-minutes-160002081.html)). Hold each clip to one feature, as Duolingo Chess did with self-contained scenes that cut down cleanly ([Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video)).

## Audio: licensed music, UI sounds, and your own voice

**Use music in the hero film and no voiceover.** That matches Sill and the dominant 2023–2026 pattern (Notion 3.0, v0, Figma Config), and it survives muted autoplay ([Content Cabinet](https://www.contentcabinet.in/insights/product-launch-video-examples); [Blare Video](https://blarevideo.com/feeds/blog/saas-product-launch-video)). Use **your own voice** in the vertical clips, at roughly 2.5–3 words per second. Typical narration runs about 150 words per minute ([Bread n Beyond](https://breadnbeyond.com/how-many-words-does-a-60-seconds-explainer-video-needs/)), and a real student founder is more credible than synthetic narration for an edtech launch. Sill's music was commissioned, which you cannot match for free. So the plan picks a stock track with a clear pulse at 100–120 BPM (a judgement call, not a sourced number), computes a beat grid offline, and snaps every headline word and camera-move start to it. Remotion has **no beat-detection API**, so run an offline onset tracker such as aubio or librosa to write `beats.json` ([Remotion docs source](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/tree/main/packages/docs/docs)). `visualizeAudio()` can drive subtle glow pulses from bass energy ([Remotion audio visualization](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/audio/visualization)).

## Upgrade the existing Remotion project rather than starting over

The repo already has `apex-scholar-video/` (Remotion 4.0.402, React 19, Tailwind v4, 1920×1080 @ 30 fps), and it is the slideshow you want to replace. It has eight fixed-length scenes (intro, six 4-second features, outro), all joined by identical 10-frame crossfades through a shared `FeatureShell`. That shell is an index label, big headline, subtitle and mock card. About 1,900 lines of hand-drawn UI mockups import nothing from `ap-prep-hub`, and the rendered MP4 has a **silent** audio track (−91 dB) ([local inspection: `apex-scholar-video/src/ApexScholarPromo.tsx`, `src/scenes/`, `out/apex-scholar.mp4`]). **Keep** `src/brand/` (`anim.ts` springs, `fonts.ts`, `BrandBackground`, `GlowCard`, `Logo`) and the font-loading `calculateMetadata`. **Retire** `FeatureShell`, the nine scene files, the manual `<Sequence>` offsets and the progress bar. Ignore the duplicate copy under `.claude/worktrees/`.

Remotion is the only tool here that renders real DOM. Motion Canvas and Revideo draw to canvas and "arbitrary HTML and CSS aren't supported", so reusing the app's UI rules them out ([pkgpulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/remotion-vs-motion-canvas-vs-revideo-programmatic-video-2026)). The integration has known traps. The app is React 18.3 with Tailwind 3.4 (a custom `color-mix` token function) and framer-motion 11.18. Remotion renders frames in parallel tabs that "don't share state", so any rAF or framer-motion animation flickers or freezes ([Remotion flickering](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/flickering)), and Remotion has no framer-motion integration ([Remotion third-party](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/third-party)). The recommended fixes are below; where Remotion's docs don't cover a step, it is an inference from the repo inspection.

| Step | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `npx remotion upgrade` (4.0.402 → 4.0.538), then add `@remotion/transitions`, `@remotion/motion-blur`, `@remotion/media-utils`, `@remotion/captions`, `@remotion/install-whisper-cpp`, `@remotion/sfx`, `@remotion/media` | Transitions such as `pushCut` need ≥4.0.500; HTML-in-canvas needs ≥4.0.455 ([Remotion presentations](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/transitions/presentations)) |
| 2 | Pin the video project to **React 18.3**; add a webpack alias `@app → ../ap-prep-hub/src` in `remotion.config.ts` | Hooks need a single React copy across the boundary |
| 3 | Drop `@remotion/tailwind-v4` and the hand-copied tokens in `src/tailwind.css`; import the app's **compiled** CSS (rebuild `ap-prep-hub` first), and set `data-theme="dark"` on the root | Carries every token and both themes with no drift ([Remotion Tailwind](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/tailwind/overview)) |
| 4 | Stub `ConfirmContext`, `AuthContext` and router (`MemoryRouter`); alias `firebase` to an empty module | Keeps renders offline and deterministic |
| 5 | Wrap framer-motion components in `MotionConfig skipAnimations`, and **check it exists in 11.18.2** before relying on it; drive all motion with Remotion `spring` / `interpolate` | Parallel-tab rendering breaks time-based animation ([Remotion flickering](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/flickering)) |
| 6 | Add an optional `controlled` prop to `SimWidget` (and a frame-driven prop to `ProjectileLauncher`); replace `AnimatedCounter` with a frame-driven counter | `useState` can't be "clicked" in a render; the state flips on a chosen frame |
| 7 | Write roughly 150 lines of primitives in one `camera/` folder: `<Camera>` keyframes (translate + scale, bezier easing), an SVG cursor rendered *inside* the camera layer, an 8-frame press/ripple, sliced-string typing, a `translateY` scroll, and a word-build headline | Remotion has no built-in cursor or zoom; this cursor approach comes from a published rule set ([daemora](https://github.com/codeandcanvaslabs/daemora/blob/982bd4a01c47eeaa314afd01e945a43640d8b3e0/crew/video-editor/rules/cursor-and-clicks.md)) |
| 8 | Register `LaunchHero` (1920×1080) and `LaunchVertical` (1080×1920) compositions; render the 4K master with `--scale=2` | Vector UI renders natively sharp at 2× ([Remotion scaling](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/scaling)) |

For flows that are painful to stage, such as live streaming from the tutor, use **Playwright** to drive `localhost:3000` with seeded data, or Remotion's **Canvas Capture** extension. Canvas Capture records at up to 30× supersampling with cursor metadata, but it requires an Apple Silicon Mac and a pinned Chrome for Testing build that gets no security updates ([Remotion Canvas Capture](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/canvas-capture)). Playwright's `recordVideo` is scratch-quality by default: it fits within 800×800, custom fps arrived only in v1.64, and Chromium bitrate was historically capped at about 1 Mbit/s ([Playwright docs](https://playwright.dev/docs/videos); [release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/npm/playwright/release/1.64.0); [issue #31424](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/issues/31424)). **OBS** (GPL-2.0) is the zero-cost fallback recorder. **DaVinci Resolve free** (exports up to 4K/60) is optional, only if you prefer mixing audio on a timeline ([Storyblocks](https://www.storyblocks.com/resources/tutorials/davinci-resolve-free-vs-studio)). Skip paid auto-zoom recorders: Screen Studio is paid, Cap Free is "personal use only", and Screenity's auto-zoom is a Pro feature ([Cap pricing](https://cap.so/pricing); [Screenity Pro](https://screenity.io/pro)). Skip Remotion Lambda as well, because it needs an AWS account with a card on file ([Remotion Lambda cost](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/lambda/cost-example)). For fonts, use the app's own Plus Jakarta Sans and Source Sans 3, which the existing `fonts.ts` already loads.

| 6. Polish and ship | Muted-on-phone QA, motion-blur passes, 4K render, unlisted YouTube upload to catch Content ID, Product Hunt gallery (1270×760) ([Screenhance](https://screenhance.com/blog/product-hunt-gallery-size)) | 1–2 days |
| **Total** | | **~2–3 weeks** |

The **reduced path takes about 1 week**. Use real components only for SimWidget, GraphWidget and StepReveal, record everything else with Playwright or OBS, and ship the hero plus three vertical clips.

## No API key is required, and two are age-gated

**The full plan runs with zero API keys.** Local Remotion rendering, local Whisper captions, local Kokoro TTS, Mixkit, Pixabay and OBS need none, and the free tools in this plan cost nothing. The only key that would add anything is optional. Check the age terms before touching any AI voice service: the Gemini API terms say "You must be 18 years of age or older to use the APIs" ([Gemini API Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). ElevenLabs bars users under 18 with no parental-consent route ([ElevenLabs ToS](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). Suno allows under-18s only with parental consent and is non-commercial on its free plan anyway ([Suno Terms](https://suno.com/terms)).

| Key | Required? | Free? | Age gate | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini API (AI Studio) for TTS | No | Yes, free tier covers 3.8 Flash TTS ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)) | **18+** | Optional and only if you are 18+. Free-tier inputs and outputs are used to improve Google products and may be human-reviewed ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)) |

| 21st.dev (cursor/zoom components) | No | — | — | Skip; write the ~150 lines yourself |

**The under-18 path** is your own voice (or Kokoro locally), Mixkit music and SFX, local Whisper and local Remotion. It touches no gated service. One finding reaches beyond this video: the Gemini 18+ term applies to the API generally, not only to TTS. If you are under 18, generating the tutor and grading fixtures through the app's own Gemini keys raises the same question for the app's backend. Settle that separately, with a parent or an adult account holder.

## Risks and open decisions

| Risk | Why it's real | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Looks like a generic AI trailer** | Sill's commenters named the style "uncanny" and a default look ([YouTube comments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJnNkfHLdFw)) | Real fixtures only, the missed-point close-up, your handwriting, a student founder voice in the verticals, an AP-specific hook, and no stock "glow" filler |
| **Overpromising AI grading** | Faked or seeded output makes the video a liability; Humane's and Google Glass's films set expectations the products didn't meet ([AutoAE](https://autoae.online/blog/best-product-launch-videos); [Content Cabinet](https://www.contentcabinet.in/insights/product-launch-video-examples)) | Show a point *not* earned; the sub "An estimate… not an official score"; the outputs on screen must be reproducible in the live app at launch |
| **Content ID claims** | Pixabay tracks can be claimed even when legal ([HeyGen Help](https://help.heygen.com/en/articles/8986144-how-to-remove-your-content-id-claim-in-youtube)) | Prefer Mixkit; keep licence files; upload unlisted 48 h before launch to surface claims |
| **CapCut terms** | Commentary reports a broad perpetual licence over uploaded content since June 2025 ([Studio L7](https://studiol7.substack.com/p/who-really-owns-your-edits-what-capcuts)); some library materials are non-commercial ([CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/clause/material-license-agreement)) | Don't use CapCut; Remotion plus Resolve covers everything |
| **College Board affiliation** | "AP" and the Bluebook look invite an implied-endorsement reading (inference) | No College Board logo; "laid out like", never "official"; trademark line on the end card |
| **UI changes after capture** | Recording a UI that is still changing is a classic failure ([Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video)) | Freeze the on-screen components in Phase 0; real components in Remotion re-render cheaply if the UI changes |
| **Integration drag** | React 18/19 and Tailwind 3/4 mismatch; KaTeX font flicker; motion-blur render time | Follow steps 2–5 above; `delayRender` for fonts; blur only on fast moves |

**Open decisions for you.** (1) **Are you 18 or older?** This decides whether Gemini TTS is even an option, and it raises the app-level Gemini question. (2) **Which tutor scenario is the hero:** "Run the Fed" in AP Macro, or a subject that better shows SimWidget at its best? Choose whichever produces the most visibly dramatic meter swing. (3) **Is GraphWidget draggable?** If not, cut the beat 5 subline. (4) **Exact numbers:** course count (37 by `AP_SUBJECTS`), sim count, and the score values in the fixture. (5) **The CTA and URL**, and whether to launch on Product Hunt (YouTube link, 30–60 s). (6) **Whether there is a real, dated feature** worth a post-credits stinger; if not, cut beat 14. (7) **30 or 60 fps.** Sill-style smoothness argues for 60, at about double the render time.

## Conclusion

The free-tools constraint turns out to cost nothing. Remotion, Mixkit, local Whisper and your own voice cover the whole pipeline legally and without a key. The real decision is about content, not tools. Sill's camera grammar is now common enough that viewers recognise and discount it. What it can't fake, and what Apex Scholar uniquely has, is a tutor that actually builds a playable thing on screen and a grader that tells a student exactly which point they lost. Build the film around those two true moments, and treat everything else as camera work around them.

The project you already have does the most important thing right: it chose Remotion. Its failure is architectural. It uses fixed-length slides, redrawn mockups and no sound, and polish won't fix that. Rebuilding it as one continuous stage that renders the app's real components gives you a better hero film. It also gives you a reusable rig, so every future feature launch becomes a new camera path through components that already exist. That turns the launch video into an ongoing distribution channel.
